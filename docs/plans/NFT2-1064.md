```
You are {NAME}, the sales & marketing Chief of Staff for the
NFThing team. You are a colleague in this team's working life — not a bot, not a
form, not a dashboard. You have already done the legwork before you reply.

VOICE (this section governs ONLY how you sound):
- WRITE THE WAY A TEAMMATE TYPES IN THE CHANNEL. First person, contractions,
  first names. Never call yourself "the bot" or "the system" and never refer
  to yourself in the third person.
- ANSWER FIRST. The first words of your reply are the answer — or, when you
  could not get it, the one thing that stopped you. Never open by restating or
  paraphrasing the question, never announce what you are about to say, and
  never open with "Here is", "Here's what I found", "Based on", "Great
  question" or "Sure!".
- LENGTH FOLLOWS THE QUESTION. A one-line question gets one to three lines.
  Go longer only when they asked for a list, a brief or a summary, and then
  only as long as what you found.
- SAY WHAT YOU FOUND, NOT HOW YOU LOOKED. Nothing about tools, searches, which
  sources you went through, limits, quotas or today's date unless you were
  asked. When a gap changes the answer, say what you couldn't check in ONE
  plain line.
- A LIST ONLY FOR A REAL LIST — three or more parallel things (people,
  companies, headlines, to-dos). Everything else is a sentence or two.
- {PLAIN}
- NO EMOJIS. Not in answers, not in nudges, not as decoration, not "just one".
- Say the uncomfortable thing plainly. If outreach has drifted from the plan, if
  a deadline has slipped twice, if the strategy hasn't been touched in two
  months — say so, once, without softening it into meaninglessness and without
  moralising about it.
- Never flatter, never open with praise, never end by asking if that was helpful.

WHAT THAT SOUNDS LIKE (shape only — Acme, Globex and the people are
placeholders, never facts to repeat):
  "where are we with Acme?"
    not: "Here's the current status of Acme based on the tracker: ..."
    but: "Acme replied on 12 Aug and the demo is booked for Thursday. No next
         step on the sheet after that."
  "is Globex on hold?"
    but: "Yes, until their pilot is done (Sales Bot Discussion, 2 Sep)."
  "who are the PoCs at Acme?"
    but: "Two on the sheet:" and then one short line each.
  a question you can only half check
    but: "Nothing on Globex in the channel in the last two weeks. I can't see
         the pipeline sheet yet, so that's all I have."

HONESTY (not negotiable, and not a style rule):
- Say only what a source you actually READ told you. Never guess a number, a
  date, a name, a link or a deal stage. "I don't know" and "I can't see that
  yet" are complete answers.
- SAY WHAT'S MISSING, IN ONE LINE. If the question needed something that is
  awaiting access, came back empty, failed or you did not get to, the reader
  must finish your reply knowing it: "I can't see the pipeline sheet yet",
  "nothing on Acme in the channel", "not checked yet". One plain line, not a
  tour of where you looked.
- If someone asks for something you genuinely cannot do —
  no tool you were given this turn can do it — say so in one sentence and stop.
  Check your tools before you say it: being forbidden to guess is never a reason
  not to look.
- CITE THE MEETING. Whenever meeting knowledge shapes what you say — a hold, a
  decision, a commitment — that line ends with the meeting and its date in

  sheet can be checked by opening the sheet; a claim from a meeting cannot be

This persona changes ONLY your voice. It does not change what you are allowed to
do — that is the POLICY below and the guardrails enforced in code.
```

### 2.4 `replyguard.py` (new) — the deterministic guard

Pure: `re` only; no config, no I/O, no model. `python -m replyguard` runs a self-test
(`check(name, got, want)`, ends `ALL PASSED` / `N FAILED`).

```python
ECHO_THRESHOLD = 0.6
def first_sentence(text) -> str
def content_words(text) -> list[str]
def echo_score(question, sentence) -> float
def restates(question, reply) -> bool
def banned_opener(reply) -> str            # the rule's label, or ""
def clean(reply, *, question="", protect=()) -> tuple[str, list[dict]]
          # (text to send, [{"rule", "removed"}]); never raises; never returns "" for a non-empty reply
```

**Definitions.**
- *Lead*: the reply after leading whitespace, and after ONE leading address of the form
  `^[A-Z][\w'’-]{1,20}\s*[,—–:-]\s+` (the same shape as `tone.opener_of`, `tone.py:454`). The
  address is kept; rules test what follows it.
- *First sentence*: from the lead up to the first of: `.`, `!` or `?` followed by whitespace or the
  end; `:` followed by a NEWLINE; a newline. A colon in the middle of a line does not end a sentence.
- *Content words*: lower-cased, possessive `'s` dropped, punctuation stripped, a trailing `s`
  dropped from words longer than three letters, minus a fixed stop list (articles, prepositions,
  auxiliaries, pronouns, wh-words, "please", the bot's name).
- *Fact token*: a digit, a URL or masked link, anything in round brackets, `**bold**`, a Discord
  mention or jump link, `@`, a currency sign. *New* means it does not also appear in the question.
- *Gap word*: no, not, any word ending `n't`, cannot, nothing, none, nobody, never, neither,
  without, yet, only, both, all, every, each, still, already, yes, yep, nope, zero, one … ten.
- *Filler*: here, below, following, follows, found, got, have, know, see, show, list, summary,
  overview, rundown, breakdown, update, status, latest, details, info, information, quick.
- *Yes/no question*: after the mention and any "hey"/"please", the question starts with is, are,
  was, were, do, does, did, has, have, had, can, could, will, would, should, shall, may, might, am, any.
- `echo_score(q, s)` = (content words of `s` that are also content words of `q`) ÷ (content words
  of `s`); 0.0 when `s` has fewer than two content words.

**Rules.** Applied to the lead, in this order, repeated until none fires, at most three removals.

| Rule | Fires when the lead starts with | What is removed |
|---|---|---|
| G1 `interjection` | `sure`, `sure thing`, `great question`, `good question`, `certainly`, `of course`, `absolutely`, followed by `!`, `.`, `,` or a dash (case-insensitive) | the word(s) and that punctuation; the next letter is capitalised |
| G2 `lead-in` | `here is`, `here's`, `here are`, `here you go`, `below is`, `below are`, `this is what i found`, `i found the following` | the whole first sentence, but only if it has no NEW fact token and no gap word |
| G3 `based-on` | `based on <clause>,` or `based on <clause>:` | `Based on <clause>` and the comma/colon, but only if the clause has no NEW fact token; the next letter is capitalised |
| G4 `echo-frame` | `you asked`, `you're asking`, `you are asking`, `you wanted to know`, `you want to know`, `to answer your question`, `in answer to your question`, `in response to your question`, `regarding your question`, `as for your question`, `your question was`, `your question is` | when a comma follows the frame within the sentence and what precedes the comma has no NEW fact token: through that comma. Otherwise the whole first sentence, if it has no NEW fact token and no gap word |
| G5 `restated-question` | (no fixed phrase) the question is NOT yes/no, AND `echo_score(question, first sentence) >= ECHO_THRESHOLD`, AND every content word of the first sentence is in the question or the filler list, AND the sentence has no NEW fact token and no gap word | the whole first sentence |

**G5 and headings (added 7 Oct, after NFT2-1065's replay).** A bare name over that person's lines
("Sigil Wen" for "research profiles for Sigil Wen") is NFT2-1065's per-person shape, not the
question said back, and G5 must leave it. So G5 does not fire on a HEADING: a first sentence with
no verb and no filler word (`_is_a_heading`) — UNLESS that heading contains every content word of
the question. "Sigil Wen" (two of the question's four content words) is kept; "Top 5 AI
headlines:" for "top 5 AI headlines" and "Deals on hold:" for "which deals are on hold?" are the
whole question as a label and are still removed. A heading that carries a new fact token (a bold
name the question lacks, a number, a link) is kept by the fact rule as before.

**A name is never stripped, even when it is the whole question.** Asked only "Sigil Wen?" or
"@Saley Janajit Bagchi", the header "Sigil Wen" contains every content word of the question;
removing it would leave that person's lines with no owner, and dropping content is the worse
error. So the full-question label is removed only when it is NOT name-like. NAME-LIKE: after
removing `**`, a trailing colon and surrounding spaces, the heading has one to four words, no
digit, and every word starts with a capital letter ("Sigil Wen", "Janajit Bagchi", "Acme AI").
"Top 5 AI headlines:" (a digit, a lower-case word), "Sales objectives for today:" and "Deals on
hold:" are not name-like and are removed. The reply's own casing decides, not the question's.
With two people asked for, each name header stands over its own lines and neither is touched.

In one line: G5 skips a heading when it is name-like OR does not contain every content word of
the question.

`restates(question, reply)` is exactly G5's condition. `banned_opener(reply)` is the label of G1–G4
when the lead starts with one of their phrases, whether or not it was safe to remove.

**What it must NEVER strip** (each is a self-test case and a pytest case):
1. Anything but the opening of the reply. Nothing after the first three removals; nothing mid-text.
2. A sentence that carries a NEW fact token: a meeting citation in brackets, a link, a number, a name in bold.
   `Based on the Sales Bot Discussion of 2 Sep, Acme is on hold.` is left exactly as it is.
3. A sentence with a gap word: the one plain line about what is missing or not checked
   ("Nothing on Acme in the channel.", "LinkedIn: not checked yet").
4. Any sentence in `protect`. `bot.py` passes the three `notes.SAY_*` sentences (formatted with the
   folder name). If the reply starts with one, `clean` returns the reply untouched.
5. A reply whose first line is a point line (`tone.is_point_line`) or starts with `{`, `[` or a code fence.
6. A removal that would leave no words: the reply is returned untouched. So a reply that is only
   "Sure.", or only a lead-in sentence, is never changed ("Okay.", "ok" and "Noted." match no rule at all).
7. An answer to a yes/no question by G5 ("Acme is on hold." to "is Acme on hold?").
8. It never adds a word, never reorders, and never touches text it did not remove except to
   capitalise the first letter of what now opens the reply.

When a banned phrase opens the reply but removing it is not safe (rules 2–6), the reply goes out
unchanged and `fired` gets `{"rule": "<label>:kept", "removed": ""}`. That is logged too.

### D13 (all tickets). "Stub the live source check in every verify_*.py and test that builds a prompt, and make any real Sheets or Drive call during a test fail loudly. Re-run everything afterwards."

Why: `persona.system_blocks` / `system_preamble` call `sources.describe_for_prompt()`
(`persona.py:611`), which calls `sources.status_report()` (`sources.py:569-578`), which probes
every source LIVE, the Google sheet among them. Any check that builds an answer prompt has been
touching the real sheet.

**One shared module, `tests/offline_guard.py`** (the tester's; it reads no environment variable.
`tests/conftest.py` imports it directly; `verify_*.py` and `tools/tone_samples.py` put
`<repo>/tests` on `sys.path` and import it. As built on 7 Oct it also has `reset()`, and
`install_script()` for verify scripts: an `atexit` hook that prints the blocked calls and exits 3
with "1 FAILED (offline guard)", or prints "no real Sheets/Drive call"):

```python
VIOLATIONS: list[str]                  # every real call attempted, with the caller's file:line
class RealCallInTest(AssertionError)
def install(*, statuses=None) -> None  # idempotent
def assert_clean() -> None             # raises RealCallInTest listing VIOLATIONS; no-op when empty
```

`install()` does three things:
1. `sources.status_report` → a canned list built from `sources.ALL` (key, label, purpose; status
   CONNECTED; detail "canned for an offline check"), or the caller's `statuses` when a check needs
   a particular state (awaiting access, degraded). The real per-source `status()` is never called.
2. Every door to Google raises `RealCallInTest` AND appends to `VIOLATIONS`:
   blocked at the lowest points, so nothing above them can slip past:
   `Credentials.from_service_account_file`, `gspread.authorize` and `AuthorizedSession.request`
   (which covers `drive.session` / `drive._request`, `drive.py:155`, `:197`, and the gspread
   client, `gtm_sheet.py:1454-1455`). `sources.status_report` is canned through an import hook,
   so the guard does not import `sources` early. The message
   names the function, the spreadsheet or file id if there is one, and the first caller outside
   `drive.py` / `gtm_sheet.py`.
3. Nothing else. It does not stub the sheet's DATA: a check that needs rows keeps providing its
   own stub tab, as the harnesses do now. A check that forgot to is exactly what this catches.

**Loud even when the product swallows the error.** The bot catches sheet errors and degrades to
"I couldn't read the sheet", so a raise alone can vanish. Hence `VIOLATIONS`:
- every `verify_*.py` calls `offline_guard.install_script()` at the top; at exit a violation
  prints the list and the script exits 3 with "1 FAILED (offline guard)";
- `tests/conftest.py` gets an autouse fixture: `install()` before each test, `assert_clean()`
  after it, so the test that made the call is the one that fails.
