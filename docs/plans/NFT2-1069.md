## 3. Challenges to the defaults (raise before building)

**C1 (D5 + D3) — four posts a day in total, and the cap of 5 can never be reached.**
"Everything else takes a spaced slot, whether it counts toward the cap or not" plus a
14:00 to 20:00 window at 120 minutes gives exactly four slots: 14:00, 16:00, 18:00, 20:00.
R8 touches and R9 now compete for them. Twenty slots a week against roughly sixteen
scheduled posts (Mon R1 R4 R7 R10 · Tue R1 R2 R5 R6 · Wed R1 R3 · Thu R1 R5 R12 ·
Fri R1 R2 R6) plus R8, R9, R11 and one re-ask per unanswered nudge. A busy week overflows,
and the overflow is always the bottom of the ranking: R1. Expect AI news to miss Mondays
routinely. `verify_s1.py`'s own Monday fixture has seven spaced candidates for four slots.
Not a reason to stop. Alternative if the team wants more room: start earlier
(`SALES_DRIP_START=12:00` gives five slots). An `.env` edit, no code.

**C2 (D2) — additive jitter is incompatible with "a full day is 14:00, 16:00, 18:00, 20:00".**
Four slots fill the window exactly. Any minute added to slot 2 pushes slot 4 past 20:00 and
rolls it. Built instead: jitter only adds time AND is capped by the slack left before the
cut-off, so it can never roll a post the plan already holds; on a four-post day it is zero.
It can still cost a slot to an item that appears later in the day (the 16:07 post is
already out when a new item needs 18:00 and 20:00). Recommendation: ship
`MESSAGE_JITTER_MINUTES=0` as the default and in both .env files, so the slots are exact.
The plan builds default 0. If the human prefers 15, it is one number in config.py and
.env.example; the mechanism is the same.

**C3 (D3) — "moves up one rank" needs a number.**
Built: effective rank = max(1, priority - rolls // 2). Two rolls is one rank, four rolls is
two. The literal reading (one rank, once) lets a rank-6 item sit at rank 5 forever behind
fresh rank 1 to 4 items, which is the starvation the rule exists to stop. Also built: R1
counts as rolled on a day it does not post, so it gets the same promotion. Both flagged for
confirmation.

**C4 (D1) — R2 is ranked above the post it screens (F4).**
At rank 5 R2 goes before R1 (rank 6) every Tuesday and Friday, so it screens the previous
day's stories; if R1 rolled the previous day it has nothing to screen. Built: R2 stays at 5
as given, and `_screen_companies` reads the most recent day with posted news in the last 7
days instead of "today, else yesterday". Alternative for Vaishnavi: put R2 at 6, where R1's
lower rule id puts it first.

**C5 (D1) — "the lower rule id" must be numeric.** R10 sorts after R9, not before R2.
Built numerically, then owner key, so two owners' groups of one rule are deterministic.

**C6 (D8) — a fixed 15:00 post costs two of the four slots.** Spaced posts must stay 120
minutes from it, so nothing spaced may land between 13:01 and 16:59. The day becomes 15:00
fixed, then 17:00 and 19:00. Only matters when Rule 13 is built; tell Vaishnavi now. The
same field is the fallback if Kushal says no to D4: `fixed_time: "14:00"` on R1 puts it back
at 2 PM, with spaced posts at 16:00, 18:00, 20:00. An edit and a restart.

**C7 (D5) — "never roll past the meeting" needs a last resort.** Built: on the last weekday
before the meeting a 5-day or 3-day touch goes ahead of every rank. If there is still no
slot (it appeared after the last slot of that day), it lapses with a log line and an audit
record, and the day-of note still goes at `MEETING_DAYOF_TIME`. It is never posted on or
after the meeting day. Alternative: post it after 20:00 that evening. Q3.

## 4. Root cause

The day's order is decided by a four-value band and then by trigger name; the two things
that override it (R1 pinned to 14:00, `rolled_first`) are respectively the least important
post in the top slot and dead code. The window fits by shrinking the gap, which is the
behaviour Vaishnavi asked to end on 6 Oct. Rolls are not stored, so "rolls to the next day"
is true only for rules that happen to run the next day.

## 5. The design

### 5.1 `bot_rules.yaml` schema

Two fields per rule, documented in the header block beside the others:

```
priority    REQUIRED. Whole number, 1 = highest. Where the rule's post goes in the
            day's order. Rules may share a number. Missing or below 1 is a STARTUP
            ERROR, like an unknown trigger.
fixed_time  OPTIONAL. "HH:MM" IST. The rule's post goes at exactly this time and
            takes no spaced slot; spaced posts keep their gap from it by moving
            around it. No shipped rule sets it.
```

Values: R1 6 · R2 5 · R3 5 · R4 3 · R5 2 · R6 2 · R7 4 · R8 1 · R9 1 · R10 4 · R11 5 ·
R12 5. Add a short comment above `rules:` naming the tie-breaks (5.3). `version:` stays 1
(additive).

Wording in the same file that becomes false and is rewritten by the builder (proposed text
in section 14, to be confirmed by Vaishnavi because `sheet_wording` is pasted into the
sheet): `global_rules.daily_cap`, `global_rules.ai_news`, `global_rules.weekends` (no
change needed, check only), R1's comment block (lines 142-164) and `sheet_wording`
(169-181), R8's and R9's comments ("must not be crowded out"), R9 `sheet_wording`
("Doesn't count toward the 5 posts a day" stays true), R3's "2 PM" (stays true: it is a
reminder).

### 5.2 Names

`item["priority"]` and `group["priority"]` stay the nextaction BAND (0 to 3). The ranking
is a new key, `rank`, on items, groups and planned messages. Do not reuse `priority` for it:
`nextaction.sort_key`, `by_band`, `drip.preview_text` and two query tools read the band.

### 5.3 The order

For every group not already sent today:

```
key = ( 0 if must_go else 1,            # an R8 touch on its last weekday before the meeting
        effective_rank,                 # max(1, rank - rolls // 2)
        0 if rolls > 0 else 1,          # a rolled item wins ties within its rank
        -rolls,                         # the longer-waiting of two rolled items first
        due_date or date.max,
        rule_number,                    # numeric part of the id; no id sorts last
        owner_key )
```

`rank` comes from the item (`_item` writes it from the rule); when an item has none
(hand-built fixtures, the weekly funnel line) `drip` looks it up by `rule_id`, then by
trigger, then uses `rules.UNRANKED = 99`.

### 5.4 The schedule

Constants: `GAP = max(MESSAGE_GAP_MINUTES, MESSAGE_GAP_MIN_MINUTES, 1)`;
`START`, `END` from config; `ON_TIME_GRACE_MINUTES = 5` (drip.py constant, not env).

1. **Anchors.** A sent row is a spaced anchor when it actually posted (`message_id` not
   null) and it is not a fixed-time post. Fixed-time means: `pinned` is truthy AND the row is
   R8 (`meeting_prep`) or its rule declares `fixed_time`. So a row written by the OLD code on
   deploy day (R1, pinned=1) still anchors. Anchor time = the row's `planned_at` on that
   day when `sent_at` is on the same date and no more than GRACE after it (or before it, as
   on a test day); otherwise `sent_at` rounded up to the minute.
2. **Blocks.** The `fixed_time` of every fixed-time group planned or already sent today.
   R8's day-of touch, reminders and breaking news are NOT blocks.
3. **place(t)**: while any block b has |t - b| < GAP, set t = b + GAP.
4. **First unsent spaced post**: `t = place(max(START, last_anchor + GAP))`. If `now` was
   passed and `now > t + GRACE`, `t = place(now floored to the minute)`. (Bot was down, or
   the kill switch was off.)
5. **Each next one**: `t = place(previous + GAP)`.
6. **Fit**: a post with `t > END` rolls (`rolled_why="window"`), and so does everything
   ranked after it. Let m be how many fit.
7. **Jitter** (only when `MESSAGE_JITTER_MINUTES > 0`): slack = END - t_m. For each fitting
   post except the day's first spaced post, draw
   `random.Random(f"{iso(day)}:{ordinal}").randint(0, J)` where ordinal = spaced anchors
   already sent + position; add `min(draw, slack left)` cumulatively. Re-run `place`. If
   fewer than m now fit, discard the jitter entirely. Jitter never subtracts and never rolls
   a post the plan holds.
8. **Fixed-time groups** (R8 day-of, `fixed_time` rules): `send_at` is their time,
   `pinned=True`, never rolled by the window, not anchors.
9. **Send order**: by `send_at`; at the same minute the fixed-time post first, then by 5.3.

The gap is never shrunk. `fitted_gap` and `slot_times` are deleted.

Defaults give 14:00, 16:00, 18:00, 20:00. One message: 14:00. Five spaced candidates: four
go, the fifth rolls.

### 5.5 Rolling and where it is stored

New table (db.py SCHEMA, beside `drip_sends`):

```
CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS drip_rolls (
    on_date     TEXT NOT NULL,     -- YYYY-MM-DD IST: the day it did not fit
    group_key   TEXT NOT NULL,
    rule_id     TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT '',
    action_type TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT '',
    why         TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT '',   -- window | cap
    meta        TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT '{}', -- JSON; R8: {"touches":[{contact_key, meeting_date, touch}]}
    UNIQUE (on_date, group_key)
);
```

- `db.replace_drip_rolls(on_date, rows)`: delete that date's rows, insert these. Called on
  every plan made by a SEND path, so the table always holds the latest plan for the day; a
  group that later fits or is sent drops out.
- `db.drip_carried(today_iso, prev_day_iso, horizon_days=14)` ->
  `{group_key: {"rolls": n, "rule_id", "action_type", "meta"}}` for groups with a row on
  `prev_day_iso`. `rolls` = that group's rows with `on_date < today`, later than its last
  `drip_sends` row, inside the horizon. Fails open to `{}` (a lost carry costs one post's
  ordering, never a duplicate).
- `drip.prev_spaced_day(day)` / `drip.next_spaced_day(day)`: Monday to Friday when
  `DRIP_WEEKDAYS_ONLY`, else every day. Friday's rolls are carried on Monday.
- `db.clear_test_day` also deletes that date's `drip_rolls`; `prune_drip_sends` prunes both.
  `wipe_operational` already empties every table not in `KEPT_ON_RESET`.

**Who writes**: `_maybe_send_drip` (after the kill switch, so a suppressed day writes
nothing, as today) and `_live_test_day`. Never `dry_run_drip`, never a question, never
NFT2-1063's answer. Not on a Sunday under `DRIP_WEEKDAYS_ONLY` (Sunday's held groups are not
rolls).

**How a rolled group comes back.** `_run_next_actions` reads `drip_carried`, and passes
`nextaction.run` two things:

- `forced_rule_ids`: carried rule ids that do not run today by weekday. They are evaluated
  fresh from the sheet (so a deliverable finished overnight is not posted), and
  `keep_forced(item)` (= `drip.group_key(item) in carried`) drops, BEFORE the contact
  dedup, any item of a forced rule whose group was not the one that rolled. `rules_run`
  says "carried over from <date>: it did not fit the posting window".
- `carried_touches`: `{contact_key: {meeting_date, touch}}` from R8 roll rows.
  `_r_meeting_prep` emits a carried touch when the meeting date still matches, the meeting
  is strictly after today and not completed, with the real "in N day(s)" count and
  `extra["carried"] = True`.

State-based rules (R1 R9 R11, and any rule on a day it runs anyway) re-enter on their own;
the carry gives them their roll count. If the fresh evaluation finds nothing, there is no
group, no new roll row, and the chain ends.

`drip.plan(..., carried=...)` puts `rolls` on each group. R1 is recorded like any other
group, so two missed days lift it to rank 5.

A day the bot is off records nothing; those items come back when their rule next runs, as
today. Stated in README.

### 5.6 Sending on time (planner addition; needed for the acceptance criterion)

F1 means a tick-driven sender posts "14:00" up to a tick late, and on this laptop up to 150
minutes late. With real send times as anchors that drift would push the fourth post past
20:00 and roll it.

- `_reminder_loop` (bot.py:5854-5869, every `REMINDER_CHECK_SECONDS`) also calls a new
  `_drip_alarm()`: one datetime comparison a minute. It calls `_maybe_send_drip()` when the
  next known send time has come (`self._drip_wake_at`, set by each plan), or, when no plan
  exists for today yet, when `drip.earliest_send_ist()` has come. It fires once per wake
  time (`self._drip_alarm_fired`).
- `_maybe_send_drip` runs under a new `asyncio.Lock` (two loops can now call it). The
  UNIQUE (on_date, slot) claim stays the last line of defence.
- The sweep tick keeps calling `_maybe_send_drip` as the safety net.

### 5.7 The sender (`_maybe_send_drip`, bot.py:7766-7921)

Order of steps after this change: test hold · sending day · `earliest_send_ist` gate ·
read `already` (fail closed) · cap log · kill switch · `planned = _plan_drip(today, already,
now=now)` · `_record_rolls` · pick the first message with `send_at <= now` that passes its
gap check · channel checks · approvals sweep (unchanged) · research · send.

- The global catch-up guard (bot.py:7843-7848) is replaced by a per-message check: a SPACED
  message goes only when `now - last spaced actual send >= GAP` to the second. A fixed-time
  message has no such check. This is what "the two-hour gap holds between every pair of
  spaced posts" is asserted against.
- One message per tick, as today.
- `self._drip_wake_at` = the earliest `send_at` still planned (for a spaced one,
  `max(send_at, last spaced actual + GAP)`), or None.
- A lapsed R8 touch (C7) is logged at WARNING and audited (`drip_lapsed`) once per day per
  group.

### 18.4 Design choices the prompt did not dictate

- **A slot is "how many spaced posts have gone today".** That one rule gives both halves of decision 1: an empty
  rule takes no slot, and a late arrival takes the next free one.
- **On time or late.** The sweep ticks every 15 minutes, so a 14:00 post really leaves between 14:00 and 14:15. A
  post that left within 30 minutes of its slot counts as on time and the next slot does not move (otherwise the
  fourth post would drift past 20:00 every day). Later than that, the next post is a full gap after the real
  send. Separately, the live sender never sends two spaced posts less than 120 minutes apart on the clock.
- **"Nothing after 20:00" is about the plan.** A post planned for 20:00 leaves on the first tick at or after 20:00.
- **All due fixed-time posts go on one tick.** One a tick would turn "10:00" into 10:00, 10:15 and 10:30.
- **"Startup error"** follows the existing convention for a bad rules file: the error is the first thing in the
  boot log, no rules load, and nothing proactive runs until it is fixed. The process does not exit.
- **The cap is decided in the day's order.** R1 is no longer decided first; the longest day is 4 against a cap of 5.

### 18.5 Known limits (each is in the report, with what could be done about it)

1. **A post that does not fit by 20:00 is not carried to the next weekday.** "As it does today": nothing was ever
   stored, and the next day's queue is worked out afresh. A weekly rule (R4, R7, R10, R11, R12) therefore waits a
   week. A normal day always fits (at most 4); it happens only after an outage or a late start.
2. **R1's window is the schedule's, not the record's.** On a day R1 posts earlier than its slot (a rule ahead had
   nothing), the stories between the real post and the slot are in neither main post. Major ones still come
   through the hourly check; all are there for a news question.
3. **A post leaves on a sweep tick**, up to `COS_FOLLOWUP_CHECK_INTERVAL_MINUTES` after its time. The laptop's 150
   would post almost at random; it must be 15.
4. **Any jitter above 0 loses the 20:00 post.** The default is 0 and the boot log warns otherwise.
5. **R11: a missed Wednesday loses that week's companies** (the bot down all day), and an 11th company in one week
   is not named.
6. **14:00 is still not a breaking-news check slot** (the `NEWS_CHECK_TIMES` default is unchanged).
